# Guidde creative brief — adapted from the winning competitor ad

Source: competitor ad `1155616497059211` (`video / talking_head_testimonial`), 40 days running, reach standing 0.9787 — selected by `score.py`.

## Objective

Drive trial signups for Guidde by making a UGC-style story ad that earns attention through narrative before revealing the product, positioned for paid social (Meta/TikTok feed).

## Audience

Individual contributors and team leads in ops, IT, support, and cross-functional roles who are the informal 'go-to' person for how things work — overworked, under-recognized, tired of repeating themselves.

## The winning formula — and why it works

The 'earned reveal' testimonial formula: 8 of 10 shots are pure story with zero product, the product appears in exactly 2 adjacent shots at the runtime's midpoint, those 2 shots are cut noticeably faster than everything around them, and the ad closes on a status claim instead of a benefit claim. It works because it disguises an ad as a personal confession long enough that the audience's guard is down when the product appears, and it makes the payoff about the viewer's standing at work, not the software's feature set — which is far more shareable and far less skippable.

## Adaptation rationale

Carried over: single handheld presenter talking direct-to-camera, a contrarian imperative hook, a mid-ad two-shot demo (capture, then artifact) placed dead-centre, an accelerating edit into the demo, and a status/leverage payoff. Changed: the scenario moves from 'surviving a micromanaging boss' to 'being the single point of knowledge-failure on a team' — a broader, less HR-risky pain that still produces the same posture (undismissable). The product beat is now explicitly Guidde (capture once, auto-generated walkthrough) instead of a competitor's UI. The payoff line is rewritten as a career-visibility claim, not a firing-avoidance claim. The weak spoken-only CTA is upgraded to a clean end card with headline, button, and URL — the one deliberate structural improvement.

## Hook

If you're the only person who knows how something works, don't just quietly panic every time you go on vacation.

## Angle

Becoming the person leadership can't overlook — not by working more, but by making your knowledge visible without extra effort, using Guidde to auto-document your process as you do it.

## Key message

You don't have to choose between doing the work and getting credit for knowing it — Guidde turns the work you already do into proof, automatically.

## Tone and casting

One presenter, mid-30s, casual and slightly wry, talking straight into a phone camera, handheld, in a lived-in home office. Conspiratorial register — talking to a peer who's also tired of being everyone's tribal knowledge, not pitching them.

## Format

9:16 vertical, ~43 seconds, phone-shot handheld UGC look, natural available light, no music bed, continuous fragment captions (2-4 words) burned in bold white sans-serif with black outline, centered, synced to speech; final 3 seconds cut to a static end card.

## Call to action

End card: Guidde logo, headline 'Turn what you know into proof,' button 'Try Guidde Free,' URL guidde.com supered cleanly — no clutter, 3-second hold.

## Production note — how the storyboard frames were made

Shots 1-9 are AI-generated visual references (`gpt-image-2`, 1024x1536). They are mood and composition guides for a director, not finished art, and they carry no text: captions and VO live as metadata beside each frame, because image models garble type.

The end card (shot 10) is deliberately NOT generated. It is a composite: the real Guidde vector wordmark (`assets/guidde_logo.svg`) over a clean plate, with the CTA set as real type on a real button in Guidde red `#CB0000`. Brand lockups are graphic elements image models cannot render reliably — they approximate letterforms and invent marks. Applying the brand as a real vector overlay is also how this works in production: the edit delivers a clean plate and the lockup goes on top as artwork.
